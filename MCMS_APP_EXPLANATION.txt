MEGAMART CENTRAL MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (MCMS)
Full Application Explanation

IMPORTANT: JAKARTA
===================
MCMS is built on JAKARTA EE 10. Jakarta is the central technology foundation
of this application. The backend uses Jakarta RESTful Web Services (JAX-RS),
Jakarta Persistence (JPA), Jakarta Enterprise Beans (EJB), Jakarta Contexts and
Dependency Injection (CDI), Jakarta Transactions (JTA), Jakarta Bean Validation,
and Jakarta Servlet configuration.

This is a Jakarta enterprise web application, not a standalone desktop program.
It runs as a Java WAR application on a Jakarta EE application server such as
Payara 6 or WildFly 28+. Its REST API is exposed under the /api path, and its
browser interface communicates with that API.


1. APPLICATION PURPOSE
======================
The MegaMart Central Management System is a browser-based central platform for
managing supermarket operations. It brings the main business activities of a
retail company into one application and one PostgreSQL database.

MCMS connects:
- Point-of-sale sales and checkout
- Product and inventory management
- Stock movement tracking and low-stock monitoring
- Suppliers and purchase orders
- Purchase-order approval and receiving
- Finance summaries and business reporting
- Customer records and loyalty information
- Employee and attendance management
- Promotions, campaigns, and marketing activity
- Notifications
- Audit logs for important actions
- Secure supplier purchase-order acknowledgement and delivery progress

The purpose is to give each department the tools it needs while keeping sales,
stock, purchasing, finance, and management information connected and
consistent.


2. WHO USES THE APPLICATION
===========================
MCMS supports role-based access. Each user signs in and sees the parts of the
application that apply to their job.

- Administrator: Full system access, configuration, monitoring, and auditing.
- Manager: Management dashboards, approvals, operational oversight, and reports.
- Sales: New sales, checkout, sales history, and customer-related operations.
- Inventory: Products, stock levels, stock adjustments, and stock movements.
- Procurement: Suppliers, purchase orders, low-stock items, and receiving.
- Finance: Finance dashboard and financial reports.
- HR: Employee and attendance management.
- Marketing: Customers, promotions, and campaigns.
- Cashier: Daily POS sales created by that cashier only; no analytics or global history.

Access is enforced on the server. Hiding a menu item in the browser is not the
only protection; backend services also verify the user's role before allowing
restricted operations.


3. MAIN USER EXPERIENCE
=======================
The frontend is a responsive single-page application made with HTML5, CSS3,
and JavaScript. The user first sees a login screen. After successful login,
MCMS displays a dashboard and a role-specific sidebar.

The application includes:
- A dashboard with sales, revenue, products, customers, employees, and stock
  statistics.
- Revenue trend charts with administrator-selectable periods.
- Top-selling product and inventory-by-category charts.
- A notification drawer and recent activity information.
- Responsive navigation that works on desktop and smaller screens.
- Toast messages and inline error messages for user feedback.
- Browser-generated receipt and multi-receipt PDF downloads using jsPDF.
- Cashier accounts open directly on New Sale and can view only their current-day sales.


4. SALES AND POINT OF SALE
==========================
The Sales module provides the New Sale screen and Sales History.

In a normal checkout workflow, a sales user:
1. Opens Sales > New Sale.
2. Selects products and quantities.
3. Applies eligible promotions when available.
4. Selects a payment method and enters the amount tendered.
5. Completes the sale.
6. Views the receipt and can download it as a PDF.

Completing a sale is transactional. The sale, sale items, payment, inventory
deduction, stock movement, loyalty update, notification, and audit information
are handled as one business operation. If a required part fails, the
transaction can roll back so that the database does not contain a completed
sale with missing inventory updates.

Cashier isolation is enforced at the database, service, REST, and frontend layers.
Each sale stores its owning cashier user in `sale.cashier_id`. The dedicated
`/api/v1/cashier` API never accepts a cashier ID from the client. Cashiers can
create sales and view only their own sales for the current day. Global dashboards,
reports, finance data, and global sales history are blocked for CASHIER accounts.
For non-cash payment methods, Amount Tendered is automatically set to the sale
total; cash payments remain editable.

The application includes demonstration-only fake payment outcomes:
- SUCCESS
- DECLINED_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS
- DECLINED_EXPIRED_CARD
- DECLINED_NETWORK

No real payment provider is contacted by these scenarios. A declined payment
returns an error and rolls back the sale and inventory changes.

Sales History supports searching and date-range filtering. Administrators can
also export multiple receipts, with one receipt per PDF page.


5. INVENTORY MANAGEMENT
=======================
The Inventory area manages the products that MegaMart sells.

Users with the appropriate role can:
- Create, update, and remove products.
- Organize products by category.
- View prices, descriptions, current quantity, and stock limits.
- Adjust stock when an authorized correction is needed.
- View a product's stock movement history.
- Identify products that have reached or fallen below their minimum stock level.

Stock movements provide a record of quantity changes, including sales,
adjustments, and received purchase orders. Low-stock notifications help the
business react before products become unavailable. A scheduled Jakarta EJB
Timer Service job also performs low-stock reconciliation after imports or
other external updates.


6. PROCUREMENT AND PURCHASE ORDERS
==================================
Procurement connects low inventory levels to supplier ordering.

The purchase-order workflow is:
1. A procurement user chooses a supplier and adds products to a new order.
2. The order is saved as DRAFT.
3. The procurement user submits it for approval.
4. The status changes to PENDING_APPROVAL.
5. A manager or administrator approves or rejects the order.
6. An approved order can be received.
7. Receiving increases inventory and records stock movements for its lines.

This workflow provides separation of duties: the person preparing an order does
not automatically approve it. Unauthorized approval attempts are rejected by
the backend.

Approved purchase orders are placed in a transactional PostgreSQL email outbox.
A scheduled EJB worker retries delivery through Brevo SMTP. A supplier receives
an acknowledgement link and a separate delivery-progress link. Both pages make
no database-changing request on load; an explicit human button click sends a
POST request. Progress can be set to SHIPPED or DELAYED and is immediately
visible to procurement, managers, and administrators through the purchase-order
status and WebSocket notification.


7. FINANCE AND REPORTING
========================
The Finance Dashboard summarizes business income and purchasing expenses. The
general dashboard also shows sales trends and operational totals.

The Reports area provides business data for authorized users. Reports can be
viewed as JSON and downloaded as CSV where supported. Reporting helps managers,
finance users, inventory staff, and procurement users make decisions from the
same operational data used by the rest of the application.


8. CUSTOMERS, HUMAN RESOURCES, AND MARKETING
============================================
Customer management stores customer records used by sales and marketing. The
sales process can associate customer and loyalty information with transactions.

Human Resources provides employee records and attendance functionality for HR,
managers, and administrators.

Marketing users can manage:
- Promotions linked to real inventory products
- Campaigns with dates, budgets, descriptions, and statuses
- Social marketing posts

Active promotions can be made available in the point-of-sale flow, connecting
marketing decisions directly to real products and customer purchases.


9. NOTIFICATIONS AND AUDITING
=============================
MCMS generates notifications for relevant operational events, including
low-stock conditions and other business activity. Users can see notifications
on the dashboard or open the notification drawer.

Audit logs record important actions and provide administrators and managers
with a history of changes and workflows. This makes it easier to investigate
what happened during sales, inventory changes, purchasing, approvals, and other
protected operations.


10. SECURITY
===========
Authentication uses a custom HMAC-SHA256 JSON Web Token (JWT) utility and a
security filter. Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes rather than plain text.
After login, the browser sends the JWT with API requests.

Authorization is checked in the backend service layer through the current user
and role context. The system distinguishes authentication failures from
forbidden operations and returns appropriate API errors.

JWT logout revocation is supported, supplier API requests are rate-limited, and
supplier identity is bound to a supplier claim. Confirmation and progress links
use short-lived HMAC-signed tokens, exact database token matching, expiry checks,
and single-use acknowledgement token clearing.

Production deployments must provide a strong MCMS_JWT_SECRET environment
variable of at least 32 characters. The development fallback secret must not be
used in production.


11. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
==========================
The application follows a layered Jakarta architecture:

- Browser frontend: HTML, CSS, and JavaScript single-page application.
- REST resources: Jakarta JAX-RS endpoints under /api.
- Services: Business rules, workflows, transactions, and role checks.
- Repositories: Database access and query operations.
- Entities: Jakarta JPA mappings for business data.
- DTOs: Structured request and response data between the API and frontend.
- Security: JWT authentication, request filtering, and role authorization.
- Messaging reliability: transactional email outbox with retry worker.
- Database: PostgreSQL relational storage.
- Application server: Payara 6 or WildFly with Jakarta EE 10 support.

JTA transactions are especially important for checkout and purchase receiving,
where several related records must succeed or fail together. CDI and EJB manage
injection and service lifecycles, while Bean Validation checks incoming data.

13. API AND DEPLOYMENT
======================
The project is packaged as a WAR file named mcms.war. The usual local context
root is /mcms, giving the application URL:

http://localhost:8080/mcms/

The REST API base path is:

/mcms/api

Important endpoint groups include authentication, health, dashboard, products,
sales, purchase orders, customers, suppliers, finance, HR, marketing, reports,
notifications, and audit logs.

Cashier endpoints:

- `GET /mcms/api/v1/cashier/sales`
- `POST /mcms/api/v1/cashier/sales`

Supplier portal endpoints:

- `POST /mcms/api/supplier-portal/acknowledge-order`
- `POST /mcms/api/supplier-portal/update-progress`
- `/mcms/supplier-confirm.xhtml`
- `/mcms/supplier-progress.xhtml`

The application expects a JTA PostgreSQL datasource bound to:

java:/jdbc/mcms

Health endpoints include a public liveness check and a database readiness check.
On first deployment, Hibernate can create or update the schema and the startup
seed service can add demonstration data.


14. COMPLETE BUSINESS FLOW EXAMPLE
==================================
The integrated workflow demonstrates how the modules work together:

1. A cashier completes a POS sale.
2. MCMS records the sale and payment.
3. Product stock is automatically reduced.
4. A stock movement is recorded.
5. If stock is below its minimum, a low-stock notification appears.
6. A procurement user creates and submits a purchase order.
7. A manager or administrator approves the order.
8. Procurement receives the order.
9. Inventory increases and new stock movements are recorded.
10. Finance sees the revenue and purchasing expense information.
11. Administrators and managers can review the resulting audit history.

This integrated design is one of MCMS's main strengths: departments use separate
screens, but they work from the same connected business records.

SUMMARY
-------
MCMS is a complete supermarket operations platform that centralizes retail
management in one browser application. Its most important defining feature is
that it is a JAKARTA EE 10 enterprise application: Jakarta technologies power
the REST API, persistence, dependency injection, transactions, validation,
security integration, and application deployment. The platform connects
MegaMart's staff, stock, sales, purchasing, finance, customers, HR, marketing,
reports, notifications, and audit records into one consistent system.
